### nabla

$$
\overrightarrow \nabla =  \overrightarrow i \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \overrightarrow j \frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \overrightarrow{k} \frac{\partial}{\partial z}
$$
$$
\overrightarrow\nabla = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \\\frac{\partial}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 3 usages

| Name           | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | special case                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gradient**   | the gradient takes a scalar $f$ to a vector field, for example: <br> $\overrightarrow \nabla f = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial z} \end{bmatrix}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |                                                                |
| **Divergence** | Take a vector $f$ to a scalar field, for example: <br>  $\overrightarrow \nabla \cdot \overrightarrow f = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} f_1 \\ f_2 \\ f_3 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial z}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | `divergence = 0` means whatever flowing in is also flowing out |
| **Curl**       | Take a vector $f$ to a vector field, for example: <br> $\overrightarrow \nabla \times \overrightarrow f = \begin{vmatrix} \overrightarrow i & \overrightarrow j & \overrightarrow k \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ f_1 & f_2 & f_3\end{vmatrix} = \overrightarrow i \left(\frac{\partial f_3}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial z}\right) - \overrightarrow j \left(\frac{\partial f_3}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial z}\right) + \overrightarrow k \left(\frac{\partial f_2}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial y}\right)$ |                                                                |
