- Light waves travel with a speed of $c$ in a medium
$$
c=\frac{c_{0}}{n}
$$
- Optical waves denoted by $u(\mathbf{r}, t)$ should satisfy
$$
\nabla^2u-\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}=0
$$
- Optical Intensity is defined as the optical power per unit area^[The operation $\langle\cdot\rangle$ denotes averaging over a time interval much longer than the time of an optical cycle, but much shorter than any other time of interest]^[Optical intensity is sometimes also called the irradiance]
$$
I(\mathbf{r},t)=2\langle u^2(\mathbf{r},t)\rangle
$$






**Appendix**
- $I(\mathbf{r},t)$ - optical intensity - watts/cm<sup>2</sup>
- Optical power is $\mathsfit{P}(t)$ (units of watts) flowing into an area $\mathsfit{A}$ normal to the direction of propagation of light. $\mathsfit{P}(t)=\int_{A}I(\mathbf{r},t)d\mathsfit{A}$
- optical energy $\mathsfit{E}$ (units of joules) collected in a given time interval is the integral of the optical power over the time interval
